BIOGRAPHY & JOURNEY

Unfinished Yet Unbroken

A Journey of Returning, Rebuilding, and Redefining Success from Sanquelim, Goa. For most people, success is a flight ticket away. For me, success was a one-way ticket home.

31 DECEMBER 2025 · 12 MIN READ · BY YATIN SANJEEV KANEKAR

Building Success from the Soil of Sanquelim: Coming Home to Build Forward — From Ordinary Employee to Son of the Soil to Reluctant Visionary.

Coming Home to Build Forward

I spent years in the city, working at Tech Mahindra, carrying dreams that felt too big for the four walls of my apartment. The city gave me knowledge, exposure, and opportunities, but it never felt like mine. Every time I sat by the window after a long day of work, one thought echoed in my mind:

“I want to build something of my own — where my roots are.”

Like my father. Like my grandfather. Like the people before me who built not just businesses, but legacies.

The irony? Those businesses didn’t survive. Not because of lack of skill or passion, but because sometimes life folds in directions we don’t see coming. I could have taken their failure as a warning. Instead, I saw it as a foundation.

And so in 2016, I came back home to Goa, and in 2017, Infipre IT Services was born.

A Goan’s Journey of Returning and Rebuilding

Success is usually imagined as a straight road — shining, paved, and waiting for us. Mine looked nothing like that. Mine began with a return.

Not to a fancy city. Not to a strategic metro. Not to a booming tech hub.

But to Sanquelim, Goa — my hometown. A place where people said, “There’s no future here. You have to go out to make something of yourself.”

I tried that path. I went out. I worked in the city. I did everything the world told me to do.

And yet, every night after finishing work at Tech Mahindra, I felt the same question resting on my chest:

“What if success isn’t somewhere far away? What if it’s waiting for me at home?”

🌱 Why Coming Back Felt Like Moving Forward

People asked me why I would leave a stable job and growing career to come back to a small town. They imagined compromise; I saw clarity.

I noticed something unusual: Even with a smaller salary, I was saving more. Even with fewer opportunities around me, I felt more grounded. Even with uncertainty ahead, I felt at peace.

But the biggest gift? Time. Time with my parents. Time with my sister. And now, time with my wife — all of whom have been pillars in this journey. Not spectators — supporters. Not cheerleaders — contributors. Without them, Infipre would still be an idea in my notebook.

We often chase dreams across states and countries, leaving our homes behind and then blame others for changing the place we abandoned. The truth is simple — if we leave, someone else will build. Growth doesn’t wait for anyone.

So instead of complaining, I decided to participate.

🌾 Where It All Began

I come from a family where business wasn’t just a profession — it was heritage. My grandfather built. My father built. And life, as unpredictable and unfair as it is, took it all away.

We closed our businesses not because we lacked courage or ideas, but because sometimes seasons change faster than we can prepare. That failure didn’t just break our balance sheets — it broke our hearts.

I could have walked away from that story. I could have chosen a new script somewhere else. But something in me refused.

“If the legacy is broken, rebuild it. Maybe that’s where your life’s work begins.”

🧑🏽‍💻 The Ordinary Employee

Before being a founder, I was just like millions of others: an employee with a badge, a man in line waiting for the bus, a task executor inside a machine that never stops.

I wasn’t the loudest in the room. I wasn’t the fastest to rise. I wasn’t even the most confident.

I was the one who stayed back thinking, “I understand the work. But do they understand me?”

I saw brilliant people being ignored. I saw politics overpower talent. I saw shortcuts overshadow sincerity.

I didn’t want to fit in. I wanted to belong.

🌱 Sanquelim: The Unwritten Chapter

Sanquelim, Goa — my hometown — sits with quiet pride. It has been the business focal point of Goa for generations, but not many know this anymore.

It’s not branded as a tech hub. There are no skyscrapers or giant MNC campuses. But Sanquelim has history, workers, dreamers, and entrepreneurs in the making.

I set up my software firm here in 2017. People asked: “Software? In Sanquelim? Are you joking?”

But I knew something they couldn’t see: The world thinks diamonds come from cities. But diamonds are never born ready. They’re carved under pressure — quietly, invisibly, in the dark.

“When you build from where you belong, every milestone feels personal.”

⚔️ The Price of Doing It Right

Let me say this honestly: Being ethical in business is expensive.

I have lost deals because I wouldn’t lie. I have lost clients because I wouldn’t cheat. I have lost opportunities because I refused to bend rules.

Every time I sat down and asked: “Is it worth it?” — every single time, I chose yes.

If my business costs me my values, then it costs too much.

🚀 Building Beyond Tech — Building Identity

Today, my journey is no longer just one company. It encompasses an ecosystem built to serve people and businesses:

  • Infipre IT Services (Tech & Software Engineering)
  • Maya HR Solutions (Recruitment & Talent Strategy)
  • Satpurush Enterprises (Accounting & Governance)
  • Mangalkaarya / Illuminaires (Matrimonials & Social Impact)
  • Poocrafts (Art, Crafts & Creative Expression)

I am a work in progress, proudly unfinished.

🔥 To Anyone Who Feels Like Me

If you’re reading this and you feel like you are ordinary, late, tired, lost, or doubted—let me tell you this gently: So am I.

And I am still here. Learning. Living. Failing. Healing. Restarting. Believing.

“Keep going. Slow is not weak. Quiet is not invisible. Ethical is not outdated. Introvert is not incapable. Ordinary is not useless.”
