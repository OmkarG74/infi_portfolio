We Built Better Lives… But Are We Living Them?

There was a time—not too long ago—when life in a Goan home moved gently, yet meaningfully.

27 APRIL 2026 · 7 MIN READ · BY YATIN SANJEEV KANEKAR

A small house with a balcao. A backyard with a simple nhãn. Sometimes no attached bathroom. Sometimes not even a sandas inside.

And yet… life felt full.

Not because people had less. But because they needed less to feel complete.

The Life That Built Us

Mornings began with small routines.

Drawing water from the well. Waiting for your turn at the nhãn. Someone knocking on the door.

There was waiting—but no frustration.

Because waiting meant talking. Talking meant bonding.

Evenings were even more special.

If there was a TV, it was just one. Neighbours walked in freely. Children sat on the floor, elders on chairs.

One screen. Many smiles.

Meals were simple.

Cooked in earthen pots. Served hot. Shared fully.

There was no concept of “my time” or “my space.”

Everything was… ours.

The Truth About Our Parents

Our parents were not laid back.

Goans. Indians. They have always been hardworking.

They woke up early. Managed homes. Handled jobs, farms, businesses. Balanced responsibilities without complaint.

They didn’t have luxury. But they had strength, discipline, and clarity.

And most importantly, they had one dream:

“Our children should have a better life than us.”

When Love Quietly Turns Into Pressure

And so it began.

Better schools. Better facilities. Better exposure. Better opportunities.

Nothing wrong.

But somewhere, “better life” slowly became:

  • Bigger achievements
  • Higher salaries
  • More comfort
  • More success

And silently, one more layer got added…

“My child should achieve what I could not.”

From One ‘Nhãn’ to Many Rooms

The child grows.

From a house with one nhãn… to homes with multiple bathrooms. From one shared TV… to screens in every room. From walking together… to individual vehicles for everyone.

We removed inconvenience.

But along with it… we removed interaction.

Earlier, one nhãn meant everyone crossed paths daily.

Today, five bathrooms mean… we don’t even see each other.

Earlier, even without a sandas inside, people felt secure.

Today, with everything inside the house… something still feels missing.

Easy Money, Busy Lives, Silent Homes

With globalization, life has changed.

Opportunities increased. Income improved. Work expanded beyond time zones.

And this is good.

A growing nation needs hardworking people. We are not meant to be idle. We are meant to progress.

But in this progress…

Our days became fuller. Our homes became quieter.

Parents are busy building the future. Children are busy preparing for it.

And somewhere in between… the present is slipping away.

The Question We Avoid Asking

We say we are doing everything for our children.

But pause for a moment and ask:

  • Are we pushing them to grow… or to fulfill our unfinished dreams?
  • Are we preparing them for life… or protecting them too much from it?
  • Are we giving them comfort… or making them too comfortable?

Because the real world is not always easy. And a child who only knows comfort may struggle with reality.

At the same time, a child who only knows pressure may forget how to live.

The Strange Pull Towards Simplicity

That’s why today, despite everything, we see a shift.

People wanting:

  • Food cooked in clay pots
  • A simple cup of chai
  • Quiet evenings with family

Not because they lack options. But because they are searching for something deeper.

Something they may not even have experienced fully… but somehow still miss.

What Did We Really Lose?

We didn’t lose facilities.

We lost:

  • Unplanned conversations
  • Shared routines
  • Everyday togetherness

We gained comfort. But we reduced connection.

A Balance We Must Relearn

This is not about going back.

We don’t need to remove bathrooms or switch off all screens.

This is about awareness.

About knowing:

  • When to push… and when to pause
  • When to provide… and when to let them experience
  • When to work… and when to sit together

Because a good life is not just about success. It is about balance.

Ending Thought

Our parents gave us everything they could. Not perfectly. But honestly.

Now, we are doing the same.

But maybe, just maybe… we need to ask one simple question:

Are we raising successful children… or content human beings?

Because in the end…

A child doesn’t remember how many rooms the house had.

“They remember… who was there with them inside it.”
